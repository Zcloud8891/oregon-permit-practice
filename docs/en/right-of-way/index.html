Right of way in Oregon: who goes first

Right-of-way questions are some of the most common on the Oregon knowledge test. The law says who must yield; it never gives anyone the right to force their way through. Never assume the other driver will yield to you.

Intersections with stop signs

Stop at the stop line or crosswalk. If there is none, stop before the area where people would cross. At an all-way stop, the usual courtesy is that the first driver to stop goes first; if you are unsure, let the driver on your right go. At a two-way stop where drivers face each other, a driver turning left waits for oncoming traffic going straight.

Intersections with no signs or signals

Look left, then ahead and right, and be ready to stop. Yield to any vehicle already in the intersection or coming from your right. If your road ends at a T-intersection with no signs, drivers on the through road go first.

Even on a green light, do not enter an intersection unless there is room for your car on the other side.

Roundabouts

Traffic in a roundabout moves counterclockwise. Slow down, stop for pedestrians in the crosswalk, and yield to vehicles already circling and those leaving. Wait for a gap, merge, and signal right when you exit. Never pass a bicycle or a large truck inside a roundabout. If an emergency vehicle is coming, do not enter; if you are already inside, keep going, exit, then pull over.

Pedestrians and crosswalks

Every intersection has a crosswalk, whether or not lines are painted. You must stop for a person crossing in a crosswalk who is in your lane, the lane next to yours (including a bike lane), or the lane you are turning into. Never pass a car that has stopped at a crosswalk. A person using a white cane or guide dog always gets the right of way; stay stopped until they have crossed the whole road, even if your light is green.

Oregon Driver Manual pages: 42, 43, 46, 47, 48 (Oregon Driver Manual 2026-2027, Form 735-37 (1-26)).

Common questions

Who goes first at a four-way stop if two cars arrive together?

Let the driver on your right go first. Otherwise, the first car to stop normally goes first.

Do I have to stop for people at a crosswalk with no painted lines?

Yes. Unmarked crosswalks exist at every intersection, and you must stop for a person crossing there just as at a marked one.

Do I signal when leaving a roundabout?

Yes. Use your right turn signal to show you are exiting, and watch for pedestrians in the crosswalk.
