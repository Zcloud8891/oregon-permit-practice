Oregon road signs: what the shapes and colors mean

Many questions on the Oregon permit test show a sign and ask what it means. You do not need to memorize every sign. If you know what each color and shape stands for, you can work out most of them at a glance.

Red signs: stop or do not do something

Red is used for signs that forbid an action. The eight-sided stop sign means a full stop every time. After stopping, give the right of way to vehicles and to people in a marked or unmarked crosswalk, then go when it is safe.

The upside-down triangle is a yield sign: slow down, let others go first, and stop if you need to. A red-and-white square Do Not Enter sign tells you that you are about to go the wrong way. A red circle with a slash over a picture means the action shown, such as a U-turn, is not allowed.

White rectangles: rules you must follow

Black-on-white rectangular signs are regulatory. They state a rule that applies where the sign stands: the speed limit, one-way traffic, which lanes may turn, no turn on red, carpool (HOV) lanes and similar rules. Some speed limit signs are electronic and change with conditions.

Yellow signs: a warning about what is ahead

Yellow means caution. Warning signs tell you about curves, merging traffic, a lane that ends, a signal or stop sign ahead, a roundabout, deer, slippery pavement, a steep hill or low clearance. The five-sided yellow sign marks a school zone. Slow down and be ready to react.

Green, blue and brown signs: information

  • Green: directions, distances and exit numbers.
  • Blue: services for drivers, such as gas, food, lodging, rest areas, EV charging and hospitals.
  • Brown: parks, recreation areas and historic or cultural sites.
  • Safety Corridor signs mark highway stretches with more serious crashes than average; turn your lights on there.

Oregon Driver Manual pages: 7, 8, 10, 13, 14 (Oregon Driver Manual 2026-2027, Form 735-37 (1-26)).

Common questions

How can I tell what a sign means if I do not recognize it?

Look at the color and shape first. Red means stop or a prohibited action, yellow is a warning, white rectangles state a rule, green gives directions, blue lists services and brown points to recreation or history.

Do I have to obey a sign if a police officer waves me through?

No. The manual says to obey signs, signals and markings unless a police officer or road worker is directing traffic. Their directions come first.

What do brown road signs mean?

They point you to parks, picnic areas, scenic spots and historic or cultural places. They are information only.
