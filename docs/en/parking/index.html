Parking and stopping rules in Oregon

Expect a question about how far to park from something. Here are the places Oregon forbids stopping or parking, with the distances from the manual.

Never stop or park

  • In a traffic lane, inside an intersection, or double-parked next to a parked car.
  • On a sidewalk, crosswalk, bike lane or path.
  • On railroad or light rail tracks, a bridge, overpass or tunnel.
  • Between the two roads of a divided highway, or in front of a driveway.
  • Where signs, traffic control devices, painted diagonal stripes or other pavement markings forbid it.

Distances to remember

  • 10 feet from a fire hydrant.
  • 15 feet from a fire station driveway on the same side (75 feet across the street).
  • 20 feet from a crosswalk at an intersection, marked or not.
  • 50 feet from a sign or signal your car would hide, and 50 feet from the nearest rail of a crossing.
  • 12 inches or less from the curb when parallel parked.

Parking on hills

Always set the parking brake and leave the car in park (or in gear with a manual). Turn the front wheels so the car would roll away from traffic: facing downhill, turn them toward the curb; facing uphill with a curb, turn them away from the curb; with no curb, turn them toward the edge of the road.

Disabled parking and breakdowns

Parking in a disabled space without a valid permit can cost $165 to $1,000, even for a few minutes. If your car breaks down, get as far off the road as you can, turn on hazard lights, and warn traffic if your car cannot be seen from 200 feet.

Oregon Driver Manual pages: 68, 69, 70, 71 (Oregon Driver Manual 2026-2027, Form 735-37 (1-26)).

Common questions

How close to a fire hydrant can I park in Oregon?

No closer than 10 feet.

Which way do I turn my wheels when parking uphill with no curb?

Toward the edge of the road, so the car would roll off the road rather than into traffic.

Can I stop on a bridge if I am only waiting a minute?

No. Stopping or parking on a bridge, overpass or in a tunnel is not allowed except in a real breakdown.
