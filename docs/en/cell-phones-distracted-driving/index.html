Cell phones and distracted driving in Oregon

Distraction is a leading cause of crashes, and Oregon has strict phone rules, especially for new teen drivers.

Phone rules

Drivers under 18 may not use a cell phone or any mobile electronic device while driving, not even hands-free. Turn it off or put it in the back seat. Drivers 18 and older may use a device only through a hands-free accessory that needs no more than a quick touch of one finger to turn on or off.

Other distractions

  • A TV, tablet or video screen must not be visible to the driver.
  • Music so loud it can be heard 50 feet away is illegal; you need to hear horns and sirens.
  • Eating, makeup, reading or texting take your eyes off the road.
  • Do not hold a person, pet or package in your lap. Pull over to deal with passengers or pets.

Fatigue and road rage

Yawning, heavy eyelids or drifting from your lane are signs you are falling asleep; stop and rest. If another driver is aggressive, stay out of their way and do not challenge them.

Oregon Driver Manual pages: 80, 81 (Oregon Driver Manual 2026-2027, Form 735-37 (1-26)).

Common questions

Can a 16-year-old use a phone on speaker while driving in Oregon?

No. Drivers under 18 may not use a mobile device at all while driving.

What counts as hands-free for adult drivers?

An accessory that needs only minimal use of a finger to turn the device on or off.

Is it legal to play music loudly in Oregon?

Not if it can be heard 50 feet from your vehicle.
