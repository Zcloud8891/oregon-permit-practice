Following distance and your space cushion

The Oregon Driver Manual asks you to keep a "space cushion" of empty space in front of, beside and behind your car. That space is the time you have to react.

How much space to leave

A safe following distance is 2 to 4 seconds. Above 30 mph, leave 4 seconds or more. To check, watch the car ahead pass a fixed point such as a pole, then count seconds until you reach it. If you get there in under two seconds, drop back and count again.

When to leave even more room

  • Wet or slippery roads, bad weather or darkness.
  • Behind motorcycles, bicycles, or large vehicles whose drivers cannot see you.
  • When pulling a trailer or carrying a heavy load.
  • When someone behind you wants to pass.
  • On a hill, where the car ahead may roll back, and in work zones.
  • While you are still learning to drive.

Space beside and behind you

Avoid driving right next to other cars on multi-lane roads, keep away from parked cars whose doors may open, and make room for cars merging onto the freeway. If someone is following you too closely, move right if you can; if not, slow down gradually so they can pass. Never brake hard to punish a tailgater.

Stopping distance

The faster you go, the farther you travel before stopping. At 60 mph, the distance from seeing a hazard to a full stop is longer than a football field.

Oregon Driver Manual pages: 26, 27, 28, 29 (Oregon Driver Manual 2026-2027, Form 735-37 (1-26)).

Common questions

What is the following-distance rule in Oregon?

Leave 2 to 4 seconds behind the vehicle ahead, and at least 4 seconds when going faster than 30 mph.

How do I count my following distance?

Pick a fixed object the car ahead passes, then count "one thousand one, one thousand two..." until your car reaches it.

Why leave more room behind a truck?

The driver may not see you, and the truck blocks your view of the road ahead.
